    Why harmonize?

    NASA has watched fires from space with MODIS (on the Terra and Aqua satellites, 1 km pixels) since 2000, and with VIIRS (375 m pixels) since 2012. VIIRS sees smaller fires, so it reports several times more hotspots. MODIS is near the end of its mission, and VIIRS is its successor.

    If you simply join the two records, the change of sensor looks like an explosion of fire in 2012.

      How it works

      1. Clean. Keep only vegetation fires (no volcanoes, industrial heat sources or offshore flares) with nominal or high confidence, and drop duplicates.
      2. Assign. Place each of the detections in its state, biome and the Campinas Metro Area using IBGE boundaries.
      3. Fill the Terra-only years. Until mid-2002 only Terra flew. We scale those months by the Terra-to-(Terra + Aqua) ratio measured in 2003–2011, separately for each calendar month and region.
      4. Calibrate. Both sensors flew together from 2012 to 2025 (— clean months). For every region we fit a model that converts MODIS counts into VIIRS-equivalent counts, then apply it backwards to 2001–2011 with a 90% prediction interval. We tested five models, from one ratio for all of Brazil to a regression per region, on years each model had not seen, and kept the most accurate. It has three parameters per region, so it is easy to check and to rerun.
      5. Check. Models are trained on 2012–2018 and tested on 2019–2025, and the other way round. Months with reduced satellite coverage are left out of training and flagged in the calendar; when VIIRS has a gap, the calibrated MODIS value fills it.

      Limits

      • Hotspots count active fire pixels at the moment a satellite passes. They are not burned area, and cloud cover hides fires.
      • Values before 2012 are estimates. Their uncertainty is shown and grows in regions with few fires.
      • Satellite orbits drift over time, which changes the hour of observation. The VIIRS-to-MODIS ratio fell slightly after 2022, and the 2019–2025 test captures this.
      • 2026 is not included yet: NASA publishes the yearly archive after each year ends.

      Data: NASA FIRMS active fire archive (MODIS Collection 6.1, VIIRS S-NPP 375 m), accessed November 2026. Boundaries: IBGE via geobr.
